# The name

The **code** is GPLv2: copy it, change it, redistribute it, sell it. That
freedom is the point, and nothing here takes any of it back.

The **name** is separate. "Habumi" identifies this project and the builds that
come from it. A fork is free to exist — it is not free to call itself Habumi.

The reason is not possessiveness. If two different programs both call themselves
Habumi, a user who hits a bug in one has no way to know which project to report
it to, and no way to know whether the thing they downloaded is the thing they
read about. Keeping the name attached to one source is what makes a bug report
mean something.

## What that means in practice

**You may**, without asking:

- fork the project, modify it, and distribute your version under a different name;
- say truthfully that your work is *based on Habumi*, *a fork of Habumi*, or
  *compatible with Habumi*;
- use the name to refer to this project — in articles, comparisons, packaging
  metadata, documentation.

**Please don't**, without asking:

- name your fork or your product "Habumi", or something close enough to be
  mistaken for it;
- present a modified build as if it were an official release.

## Declared, because it changes how much this is worth

**The name is not a registered trademark.** No registration has been filed with
any office. This document states an intention and a request; it is not the same
thing as a registered mark, and it should not be read as one.

**What was actually checked, on.** A web search found no registered
mark called "Habumi" surfacing from the USPTO, EUIPO or Italian UIBM records,
and no software project with that name. The word does exist as a personal
nickname and as a handle on several social platforms — which creates no
trademark rights in classes 9 or 42.

**What was not checked, and it matters.** Those registers are queried through
forms that search engines do not index record by record, so "nothing found" is
weak evidence about what is actually registered. A real clearance search also
covers marks that are *similar* in spelling or sound, not just identical ones —
and there the nearest thing is concrete: **HABU** is a registered mark, held
among others by an entity in the scientific and technological services class,
which is this project's class. "Habumi" is "HABU" plus two letters, and
trademark law asks about confusion, not identity.

So: no conflict is known, one plausible neighbour is known, and a professional
clearance has not been done. That is the honest state of it.
